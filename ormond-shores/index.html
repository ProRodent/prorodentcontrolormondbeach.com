Salt, humidity and exterior metal.

Air near the Atlantic can carry salt, and salt speeds corrosion on thin metal. A vent cover that looked sound last year may show rust at the corners now. A flimsy screen on a gable vent may fail in a storm. Each failure is a possible entry.

  • Look for rust or bent edges on vent covers, soffit screens and flashing.
  • Press on exposed screens to feel for loose frames.
  • Check that pipe and cable collars are still tight.
  • Photograph any area where metal has flaked or lifted.

Storm season and cleanup.

Hurricane season brings wind, rain and debris. Storms do not cause rodent problems alone, but they can strip a loose cover, push animals toward dry shelter and leave debris against a wall. After a storm, walk the building before you start cleanup and look for anything that changed.

If you clear debris, wear gloves and keep dust down. Fallen limbs can hide old droppings, and disturbing them raises dust.

How a visit unfolds.

The technician starts outside, walks the roof edge and vents, then checks the attic or ceiling, then the garage. Traps go on active lanes, and sealing uses metal built for damp, salty conditions. A return visit tests each seal.

Safety first.

Leave droppings alone, keep pets and children out and use the CDC steps below if you clean. Do not set bait, since an animal that eats it can die inside a wall.

Bridging the gap to the visit.

  • Keep brooms and vacuums well away from droppings.
  • Leave the bleach in the cupboard until the scope of the job is clear.
  • Set a coin next to each gnaw mark when you photograph it to show scale.
  • Estimate how long ago the signs started.

Short version of the public health advice, with the original pages linked after it.

  • Aged pellets disintegrate when brushed, which is why a light mist of disinfectant comes before any towel or hand.

  • Disposable gloves are the best choice. Finish with a thorough soap-and-water wash.
  • Disinfect first and collect second. The soak keeps dust from lifting while you wipe.
  • Knot the bag and drop it in the ordinary household bin.
