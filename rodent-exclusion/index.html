What counts as an opening.

Roughly a quarter inch admits a mouse, and roughly half an inch admits a rat. That is smaller than most owners expect. The openings we find most often are not dramatic: a gap around a pipe, a worn corner of a garage door, an uncapped vent, a cable passing through siding.

Why the metal matters.

Salt air is a fact of life near the Atlantic. It corrodes thin aluminum and cheap hardware, and rust opens new gaps. Stainless mesh, galvanized flashing and hard-cure sealant last longer in these conditions. We check what is already installed and replace parts that have started to fail.

Mesh must be fastened, not just pushed in. A rodent will pull out loose steel wool and use the hole it plugged. Fasteners, flashing laps and sealed edges are what separate a repair from a delay.

The right order.

Sealing begins after trapping shows which routes are active. Closing a hole too early can lock an animal inside, which makes the noise worse and sometimes leads to a dead animal inside a wall. The sequence protects you from that.

  • Walk the building and list every opening.
  • Trap along active lanes until catches stop.
  • Seal the openings, roof first and then the ground level.
  • Return to tug-test each seal and record the result.

Trees, fronds and the roofline.

Live oaks and palms are common here, and a limb or frond that touches the shingles is a bridge. Exclusion covers the building, but the bridge has to be trimmed too. What you can do meanwhile.

  • Close the door of the noisiest room and leave it closed.
  • Pellets stay exactly where they fell, since a broom throws them into the air.
  • Lay out no poison, since an animal that eats it often retreats into a wall cavity and dies.
  • Note the clock time whenever the sound begins.

Before you call, set the broom aside.

Fine dust is the hazard, so every step below is about keeping it out of your breathing zone.

  • Sweeping and vacuuming dry droppings are off the table, since both fling fine particles toward your lungs.
  • Airing comes first. The CDC starts closed-space cleanup with roughly thirty minutes of fresh air.
  • Cover your hands throughout the task and keep even gloved fingers from touching your face.
  • Wet everything thoroughly, give it a few minutes, then lift it with paper towels.
  • Knot the bag and drop it in the ordinary household bin.
  • If you feel off later, tell a doctor about the cleanup and let them judge what matters.

Sealing, your questions answered.

Is expanding foam enough to plug a hole?

No, because rodents gnaw through it quickly. In some spots it works as filler behind metal, but never as the barrier itself.

Does salt air affect sealing?

Metal near the coast tends to corrode faster. We favor heavier stock and check existing screens and vent covers for rust.

How do you know every entry is found?

We cannot promise perfection. We search the roof edge, vents, utilities, slab and doors, and the return trip catches anything new.

Do you trim trees?

We note limbs and fronds that touch the roof and tell you what needs trimming. Tree work belongs to a tree service.

Can I seal gaps myself?

Small jobs are possible, but do not close a hole while an animal is still inside. Ask the technician which gaps you can handle.
