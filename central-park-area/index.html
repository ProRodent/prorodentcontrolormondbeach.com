Green edges, in general.

Open grass, shrubs and planted beds provide cover for small animals. A yard that borders a larger planted area may see more traffic along fences and walls. We cannot say whether that applies to any specific home. It is a reason to walk the border and note what runs up to the building.

  • Check where shrubs, vines or beds touch the siding.
  • Look for runs in the grass along a fence or wall base.
  • Note any trash bin, compost or feeder near the edge.
  • Photograph fresh holes or soil heaps.

Heat, rain and timing.

Summers in Ormond Beach are hot and wet, and storms are frequent. Rain pushes burrowers toward dry shelter, and heat sends others to shade. A walk after a storm shows what changed, and it takes ten minutes.

Seasonal homes and rentals.

Coastal cities often include part-time residents and vacation rentals, and we do not know how many homes here fit that description. A house left empty for weeks gives animals time to settle. Before a long absence, empty open food, seal pantry goods and ask someone to look inside.

Rodent damage grows every week, so calling early usually costs less than repairing later.

We walk the outside, check the attic or ceiling space and inspect the garage or storage room. Traps follow the evidence, and sealing closes the entries once animals are out. A return trip tests the work.

While you wait.

Pellets stay where they are, children and pets stay away, and any cleaning follows the CDC steps in the box below. Skip bait and glue boards.

While you wait for the visit.

  • Toddlers and inquisitive pets should stay clear of pellets, shredded nests and bitten cords.
  • A shed or garage closed up for weeks needs its doors propped open and some time to air.
  • If you have already set a trap, do not move it.
  • Mention any storm cleanup, roof work or palm trimming done lately.

Before you call: keep the dust down.

These lines paraphrase public health pages and stay general. For medical questions, a doctor is the right contact.

  • Slow, gentle wiping keeps dust down. Fast scrubbing lifts it.
  • Think of the first thirty minutes as ventilation time with no one inside.
  • Because hands move waste toward the mouth, gloves start the job and soap ends it.
  • Disinfect first and collect second. The soak keeps dust from lifting while you wipe.
  • Knot the bag and drop it in the ordinary household bin.
  • Tell your doctor about rodent contact if illness follows within weeks. We deal with pests and nothing else.
