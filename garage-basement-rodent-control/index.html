What makes a garage so attractive.

A garage offers shade, quiet and shelter from rain, and it is rarely checked. Totes, tool chests, camping gear, spare appliances and old paint cans create cover. Cardboard and fabric supply bedding. Pet food and seed bags provide a meal.

Add the door itself. A rubber seal that has flattened or split leaves a thin line of light at the base, and a thin line is enough. Corners, where the door meets the track, are another favorite.

A walk along the wall line.

We start outside and look at the door seal, the threshold and any vents. Inside, we follow the walls with a flashlight, checking shelving, behind appliances and the edge where wall meets slab. Pellets and grease marks show where animals travel.

  • The bottom of the door, both side seals and the top corners.
  • Spots where pipes, wires or cable pierce the wall.
  • Shelving, totes and stacks of cartons.
  • The ceiling over stored items, in case of droppings or stains.
  • A connected utility room, water heater closet or shed.

Closing the door.

Sealing a garage often means a new door seal, a rebuilt threshold and metal over wall penetrations. If the garage connects to the house, we also check the shared wall and any door into the living area.

Our exclusion service covers the materials. For animals already inside, trapping comes first, so nothing is sealed in.

A seasonal note.

Ormond Beach is a coastal city with visitors and part-time residents. A garage that sits closed for weeks while owners are away is quiet and undisturbed. Before a long absence, empty any food storage, check the door seal and ask someone to look inside now and then. On return, open the door and look before you start unloading.

While you wait for the visit.

  • Move whatever blocks the garage wall, the cupboard below the sink and the ladder to the attic.
  • Chewed cardboard stays unshaken, because shaking sends dust skyward.
  • If you buy a snap trap, put it somewhere a toddler or a large dog cannot get to.
  • Decide which room smells worst and how you would describe the smell.

Before you call: a careful way to treat pellets and nests.

No visit is required to follow this routine, and the steps are the same for a garage, an attic or a kitchen cabinet.

  • The order is wet first and dry never.
  • Prop the doors open, let a breeze run through and return after about half an hour.
  • With gloves on, skin never meets droppings, urine or nesting material, and that contact is easy to prevent.
  • Saturate the waste and the surface around it, then leave it alone before the first wipe.
  • Knot the bag and drop it in the ordinary household bin.
  • Anyone who becomes ill after contact with rodent waste needs a clinician, because health questions fall outside pest work.

Garage questions, answered briefly.

Do Ormond Beach homes have basements?

Few do, because of the high water table common in coastal Florida. A utility room, shed or crawl space usually plays the same role, and we treat it the same way.

How do rodents get into a closed garage?

Through a worn door seal, a gap at a corner, a vent, a pipe or cable entry, or through an open door at dusk.

Should I throw out chewed boxes?

Seal them in a bag and hold on to them so the technician can inspect the damage. Shaking or sweeping them raises dust, so avoid both.

Can stored items be treated?

Items cannot be sprayed in place. Moving them into lidded tubs, away from the wall, reduces nesting sites.

Will you check the refrigerator motor?

A spare fridge or chest freezer in the garage gets a look at its base and compressor, since the warmth there appeals to animals.
