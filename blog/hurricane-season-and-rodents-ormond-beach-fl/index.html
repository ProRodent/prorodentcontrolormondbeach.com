Storm Season and Rodents: A Walk-Around Plan for Ormond Beach

Atlantic storm season runs for months, and most of that time the sky is calm. This guide covers what wind and water actually change for rodents and how to check a property before and after a blow.

The Atlantic hurricane season officially runs from June through November. Across those months a Florida property can go weeks without a drop of storm weather, then absorb a few days of wind and rain that rearrange the yard. Rodents share that yard, and weather affects them less dramatically than folk wisdom claims.

What a storm does to the ground.

Norway rats and plenty of mice burrow. Once a burrow floods it is no longer a refuge, and the occupant moves out fast. The nearest shelter from rain might be below a deck, inside a shed or tight against the garage wall.

Roof rats behave differently. Roof rats nest high, and rising water seldom reaches them. High wind can strip nests from palm crowns, though, and an animal that loses its cover looks for sturdier shelter. Sometimes that is an attic.

Storms cannot be predicted by us, and we will not pretend call volume spikes after each squall. The narrower point is enough: wind and water open new gaps and scatter old hiding places.

Before the season: a short building walk.

Pick a dry morning in May and walk the outside of the building. A flashlight helps in shaded corners. Your goal is to find weak points before a storm exposes them.

  • Press each vent cover and screen, and note any that are loose, rusty or bent.
  • Look at the roof edge, soffits and fascia for gaps or lifted panels.
  • Check the garage door seal and the two bottom corners.
  • Find any pipe or cable that passes through a wall and look for gaps around it.
  • Photograph each side of the building so you can compare later.

Fix small gaps now. Large repairs, such as a damaged roof edge, belong to a roofer. Our sealing service covers the rodent side of the job.

The day after: walk before you clean.

Hold off until fallen lines and unstable limbs no longer endanger anyone. Then circle the outside slowly, low spots first. Look for fresh holes, loose screens, debris piled against walls and any limb or frond lying on the roof.

Take pictures and write down the day. Should scratching start weeks afterward, the photos help pinpoint where the animals got in. They also help if you need to talk to an insurance agent.

Debris, spoiled food and safe cleanup.

Cleanup after a storm can disturb nests and old droppings. Gloves on, dust down, and children and pets out of the room. Pellets beneath a downed limb should be left unswept. The CDC method is to wet them with disinfectant before wiping, which the safety box lower down walks through.

A power outage can spoil freezer food. Bag it tightly, store it in a covered outdoor bin until pickup and wipe the area. Open trash draws animals within a night or two.

Two walks beat one.

Treat storm season as a cycle. Give the outside one careful walk before June, then a shorter lap after any big storm. The comparison is the whole purpose, since a fresh hole is obvious against a picture of last month's wall.

  • Shoot every side of the building ahead of the season.
  • Repeat the same angles after a windy or flooding storm.
  • Jot down loose screens, cracked seals and spots where water pools.
  • Repair small defects quickly, ahead of the next weather system.

No special gear is required. Do the walk from the ground and give each side a few minutes. Climbing is for roofers, and wet or gusty weather is no moment for it.

Wet materials indoors.

Damp hangs around in attics, closets and garages once a storm passes. Wet insulation, soggy cardboard and damp wood trap odor and attract animals in search of soft nesting material. Dry or discard wet items quickly. A damp carton on the garage floor turns into perfect bedding as it dries, so replace such boxes with plastic tubs that have lids.

Shutting gaps ahead of the next system.

If your walk finds gaps, close them with metal, not foam. Coastal air is hard on thin aluminum, and rodents chew through foam in a day. Heavy mesh, flashing and hard-cure sealant last longer. If animals are already inside, call first, because sealing early can trap them in a wall.

Before you call, a plain cleanup routine.

Go line by line. Rushing is how dust ends up in the air.

  • Brooms, shop vacuums and leaf blowers all spread dust, so none of them belongs near rodent waste.
  • Airing comes first. The CDC starts closed-space cleanup with roughly thirty minutes of fresh air.
  • Disposable gloves are the best choice. Finish with a thorough soap-and-water wash.
  • Keep applying liquid until nothing looks dry, then step back for a few minutes.
  • One knotted bag can hold towels and a carcass together, with a second bag if the plastic is thin.
  • If you feel off later, tell a doctor about the cleanup and let them judge what matters.

Short answers before you pick up the phone.

Does a hurricane push rodents indoors?

Weather can shift where animals take cover. Flooded burrows and ripped-up vegetation might send them to drier places, yet they need a gap in your building before they can enter.

When should I inspect after a storm?

As soon as it is safe. Stay clear of downed lines and unstable limbs, then walk the outside of the building and photograph what changed.

My freezer food spoiled during an outage. Does that matter?

Yes. Spoiled food draws pests. Bag it tightly, move it to an outdoor bin with a closed lid and clean the area.

Should I trim trees before a storm?

Trimming ahead of the season is wise, but leave emergency work to professionals and never climb in wind or rain.

Will sealing gaps before storm season help?

It helps. A gap closed early is an entrance denied to any animal seeking dry cover.
