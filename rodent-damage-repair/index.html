Items rodents tend to chew.

A rodent incisor grows for life, so the animal gnaws without pause to wear it down. The habit harms far more than cereal boxes: insulation, flexible duct, plastic pipe, wire jackets, screens, trim and drywall can all carry bite marks.

  • Insulation: flattened, shredded or stained, usually along travel lines.
  • Ductwork: torn flexible duct leaks cooled air into the attic.
  • Wire jackets: exposed conductors raise a safety risk.
  • Screens and vent covers: chewed corners become entrances.
  • Wallboard and trim: small holes that widen over time.

Staying safe before any repair.

Chewed wiring can be dangerous. If a wire looks bare, do not touch it. If it is safe to do so, switch off the circuit and phone a licensed electrician. Our role is to document the damage and close the way animals reached it.

Documenting for an insurer or landlord.

A written plan with photographs helps if you need to explain damage to an insurance agent or a landlord. We cannot say what any policy covers, but clear records show what was found, where and when.

Older and newer buildings.

Nobody here knows how old your building is, and we will not pretend to. Houses of every age can leave gaps where utility lines enter, and wear around doors and vents is a question only an inspection answers. In every case the repair list starts the same way: what was chewed, how animals got in and what has to be closed first.

What you can do meanwhile.

  • Open the back gate for the technician and warn us about any dog that wanders loose around the property.
  • Do not trim shrubs and vines on the wall until we have looked at them.
  • Have a flashlight ready for the darkest corners.
  • Snap burrows, pellets and nest scraps on your phone before anyone walks over them.

Before you call: keep the dust down.

Use this list each time pellets, urine stains or torn nesting turn up inside.

  • Sweeping and vacuuming dry droppings are off the table, since both fling fine particles toward your lungs.
  • After weeks closed, a room needs a half hour of through-draft before any work begins.
  • Keep gloves on for the whole job, peel them off carefully and wash your hands afterward.
  • Keep applying liquid until nothing looks dry, then step back for a few minutes.
  • Put soiled towels and any dead rodent in a plastic bag, moving them with gloves or a tool rather than bare fingers.
  • Sickness after a cleanup belongs with a physician, not a pest company, so describe exactly what you touched.

Repair FAQ for homeowners.

Do you repair wiring?

We do not perform electrical work. If you find chewed wires, switch off the circuit and call a licensed electrician. We will document what we see.

Is insulation replacement included?

It is a separate line. If the cleanup shows soiled or flattened insulation, the plan lists replacement as its own item.

Do you repair roofs?

Roofing belongs to a roofer. We close rodent entry points and tell you when a roof repair is needed first.

Can I do small repairs myself?

Small jobs like replacing a vent screen are possible. Do not touch bare wire, and wait until sealing is finished.

Will my insurance cover it?

Policies differ. We cannot say what yours covers, but photos and a written plan help when you talk to an agent.
