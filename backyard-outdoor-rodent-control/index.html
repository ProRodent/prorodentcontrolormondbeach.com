The yard through a rodent's eyes.

A rodent picks a spot using three tests: is there food, is there cover, is the path safe. Fallen fruit, seed and spilled pet food supply the first. Dense groundcover, woodpiles and clutter supply the second. Fences, walls and utility lines supply the third.

We walk the property in that order. The goal is not a bare yard. It is a yard where the last few feet before your walls offer less cover than the neighbor's.

Palms, live oaks and the bridge problem.

Palms and live oaks are part of the Florida landscape here. We make no claim that either tree causes rodent trouble. The concern is a frond or limb that touches the roof, a wire or the siding, because a rat can use it as a ramp. Keeping a clear gap between canopy and building matters more than removing the tree.

Mulch is a similar story. A thin layer around plants is fine. Deep mulch piled against the foundation and over weep holes gives animals shelter next to the wall.

Sheds, decks and the underside of things.

A shed with a gap at the floor line, a deck with open lattice and a stack of pavers by the fence all provide quiet cover. Check the base of each one. Look for fresh soil, trails and droppings.

  • Look under decks and steps for burrows and fresh dirt.
  • Cap gaps at the base of sheds with metal, not foam.
  • Keep stored items off the floor of the shed.
  • Keep pet food, birdseed and fertilizer in containers that close tightly.

How outdoor work fits the plan.

Yard steps support the inside work, and they do not replace it. If animals are already in the walls, trapping and sealing come first. Yard changes then reduce the odds that new animals find the same route.

Yard FAQ for homeowners.

Will a bird feeder bring rats to the yard?

Spilled seed can. A catch tray or a habit of sweeping beneath the feeder helps, and if signs show up you can bring the feeder in for a few weeks.

Are fruit trees a problem?

Fallen fruit feeds rodents, especially roof rats. Pick it up daily and keep the ground clear.

Do I need to remove my shrubs?

Usually not. Pulling them back from the wall by a foot or more, and trimming the lowest branches, removes cover without stripping the yard.

Will you treat burrows?

We identify them, trap and close them as part of the plan. Filling a burrow before the animals are gone only moves the problem.

What about a pond edge?

If your lot backs onto a pond or drainage area, mention it. Water edges can hold cover, and the walk-through looks at that border.
