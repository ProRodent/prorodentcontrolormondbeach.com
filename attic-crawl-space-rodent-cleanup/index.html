Why cleanup waits.

Attic cleanup is a bad investment while animals still use the space. Fresh insulation gets soiled again within weeks, and the cleaning has to be repeated. Sequence matters: trapping, sealing, cleanup, and then a last visit to confirm that the space stays silent.

What we look for up there.

  • Flattened or stained insulation along paths and near vents.
  • Droppings clustered at the hatch, along beams and near the eaves.
  • Nests of shredded fiber, paper or palm material.
  • Chewed ducts, wire jackets and plumbing vents.

Attics in this climate run hot, and summer heat makes the work slower and the air heavier. We plan visits around the temperature, because a cramped, overheated space is hard on anyone working in it.

Waste handling in plain steps.

Dry droppings are mostly a hazard as dust. The CDC approach keeps things damp. Let fresh air in, glove up, drench the waste in disinfectant, give it time, then wipe with paper towels and seal everything in a bag. Brooms and vacuums are not part of that method.

The safety box on this page lists the same steps. If you choose to do a small cleanup yourself, follow them in order and stop if the job feels beyond what you can do safely.

Crawl spaces and low areas.

Plenty of Florida houses rest on a slab with no crawl space whatsoever, and others stand on piers above a shallow void. If yours has one, tell us how you reach it and whether the ground is dry. Damp soil, loose vapor barrier and open vents let rodents settle.

After the cleanup.

A last walk-through checks that the space is dry, aired and sealed. If new insulation is going in, it goes in last, so it never touches a surface that has not been treated. Photos before and after help you see what changed.

Interim measures.

  • A vehicle with debris under its hood stays parked until someone checks it.
  • Grass seed, kibble and bird food come indoors for now.
  • Bag any chewed item and save it for the technician.
  • Locate the breaker panel in case a cable was bitten through.

Before you call: cleaning waste without stirring dust.

Short version of the public health advice, with the original pages linked after it.

  • One quick pass with a broom can leave particles floating in still air for a long time.
  • Stale air concentrates whatever floats in it, so let the space breathe for about half an hour before you start.
  • Wear gloves throughout and wash up for a good while at the end.
  • Spray, wait, wipe. About five minutes sits in the middle.
  • Knot the bag, throw it away and wash your hands again.
  • Monitor your own health over the following weeks and contact a physician if you develop flu-like symptoms or difficulty breathing.

Cleanup, your questions answered.

When can cleanup start?

When no animals remain and the gaps are shut. Cleaning earlier means doing it twice.

Can I clean the attic myself?

Light jobs are possible if you follow the CDC steps below carefully. Heavy contamination, cramped spaces and sweltering attics are better handed to a technician.

Does insulation always need replacing?

Not always. Matted, stained or heavily soiled batts usually go. Clean sections may stay. The walk-through decides.

Does the odor fade afterward?

Taking out the source normally reduces it. If a dead animal is involved, see our dead rodent page.

Is cleanup priced separately?

Yes. Cleanup appears as its own line in the plan, and you can approve or postpone it.
