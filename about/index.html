What happens when you call.

A dispatcher answers at (888) 686-4821 and asks four things: the address, what you saw or heard, how long it has been going on and whether children or pets spend time in that area. Those answers decide which technician goes and what rides in the truck.

After-hours calls get a human voice as well. If nobody can arrive today, you hear so right away along with a realistic window.

The sequence at your property.

  • A technician circles the property, following the roofline and then checking attic, garage, crawl space and yard, hunting for new evidence.
  • Traps or stations are placed on the routes the signs reveal.
  • Gaps are closed using sheet metal, steel mesh and sealant that cures hard.
  • A second trip tallies catches, tests each seal and sets up any cleanup or repair.

Order is important. Closing holes too early can shut an animal inside a wall, and cleaning an attic while entries stay open means cleaning it twice.

What this site leaves out on purpose.

This site shows no founding year, license number, crew photograph, award or customer quote. Nothing of the kind reached us in a form we can verify, and an invented detail would serve you worse than saying nothing. When the technician arrives, ask for whatever proof you want.

No honest company can guarantee that a rodent never returns. Palms, oaks, ponds, fences and the vacant lot next door all keep wildlife nearby. We offer a painstaking search, long-lasting materials and a follow-up visit.

Where bait, health and expertise meet.

Federal rules restrict the sale and use of rodenticides, and the EPA page is linked at the end of the page. We would rather combine trapping with sealing. Should a bait product enter the plan, you are told its name and where it goes.

For droppings we use the CDC order: ventilate, wet, wipe, bag. Take health questions to a physician. Pest control is the only topic on which we can speak with authority.

Not sure if a mouse or a rat is to blame? Describe the pellets and the sound and we work it out.

Who performs the work.

The dispatcher schedules the trip and a technician does the work. We publish no staff names or photographs, and we do not claim any certification that we cannot document. If you want to know who is coming, ask when you book.

Work beyond rodents, like roofing, electrical repair or big drywall patches, belongs to the matching trade, and the technician says so during the inspection.

Pricing and arrival.

A quote only comes after the inspection, since a price named blind is a guess. Each stage, whether sealing, cleanup or repair, is its own line item, and you may accept or decline any of them.

Whether we can come the same day hinges on the calendar. You learn what is open before the call ends, and nothing is promised that we cannot deliver.
